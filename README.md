# Token Radar — radar-13

Pre-token protocols ranked by implied value, for make.fun's ["Build the best token launch radar"](https://make.fun/bounty/token-radar-01) bounty ($1,325 in USDC).

## What it does

Reads the sealed snapshot, scores every pre-token protocol with the radar-13 model, and writes the ranked board, the dashboard data, the dashboard files, the screenshots and `dossier.json` into the output directory. Run twice, it writes identical bytes.

## The model (radar-13)

Implied value is **calibrated from the snapshot itself at run time**, not guessed:

```
implied = exp(A) × TVL^B × categoryFactor × chainFactor × unknownAgeFactor × chainCountFactor × interactionFactor × signInteractionFactor × growth × maturity
```

- **Power law:** `A = 7.92`, `B = 0.469`, fitted by **Huber robust regression** (8 rounds of reweighting) over the 94 tokenised protocols in **board-eligible categories**. Protocols whose market cap sits far off the line (rain +6.4, concentrator −5.0 in log terms) are downweighted instead of dragging it. Market data shows the mcap/TVL multiple falls as TVL grows (0.53× at $10M TVL, 0.15× at $100M, 0.05× at $1B) — a flat 30% of TVL overstates giants ~10×.
- **Winsorisation (new in radar-8).** The log(mcap/TVL) multiple is clipped at the training set's own P5/P95 before the fit, so the one or two most extreme multiples cannot drag the line or the category factors. Ten of the 94 training protocols are clipped: five high (rain ×400, bitway-earn ×36, aave-v2 ×19, flokifi-locker ×19, anvil ×12) and five low (concentrator, grove-finance, obol, reservoir-protocol, ssv-network — all near ×0). Winsorising the fit alone moves leave-one-out 1.314 → 1.308 and leave-one-category-out 1.175 → 1.104.
- **Empirical-Bayes category factors (new in radar-8).** The ad-hoc n/(n+6) shrink is replaced by a DerSimonian-Laird random-effects estimate: the between-category variance tau^2 and the within-category variance sigma^2 are estimated from the training residuals, and each category's mean residual is shrunk by tau^2/(tau^2 + sigma^2/n). A thin or noisy category is pulled hard toward 1.0; a large, consistent category keeps its factor. EB alone moves 5-fold CV 1.248 → 1.210; with winsorisation the pair moves CV to 1.214, LOCO to 1.104 and LOO to 1.178. The change matters most for Prediction Market: rain's ×400 multiple had inflated the whole category's factor to ×2.49 under radar-7, so Polymarket was ranked #1 on the back of it; the factor is now ×1.07 and Polymarket ranks #6 on its own TVL.
- **Cleaner fit set (radar-6).** The fit leaves out the 34 token protocols in CEX, bridge, canonical-bridge and liquid-staking categories: a CEX token's value does not follow from its exchange's TVL, a bridge token is usually worth ~nothing whatever the bridge holds, and a liquid-staking token tracks its underlying. The board never ranks those categories anyway, so radar-6 calibrates on exactly the population it predicts.
- **Chain factor (radar-6).** Each chain gets a shrunk residual factor (weight n/(n+4), chains with fewer than 3 training protocols take 1.0); a row's factor is the mean over its chains. Protocols on newer, smaller ecosystems price lower at the same TVL (Kava ×0.50, Mantle ×0.57, Monad ×0.57, Berachain ×0.63; Ethereum ×0.95, Arbitrum ×1.03). Chain identity is what carries signal here — see the chain-count band factor below for what the count itself does.
- **Chain-count band factor (new in radar-10).** As a continuous linear feature the chain count carries nothing (correlation 0.09 with the residual, tested and rejected twice) — but as a binary split it does: protocols on three or fewer chains run above the fitted line (median residual +0.153 log over 59 protocols), protocols on four or more run below it (median residual −0.535 over 35), and the gap is stable across the old and young halves of the snapshot. The per-count medians are incoherent (2 chains +0.86, 3 chains −0.58), so the split is a step, not a slope: one factor per band, each band's median residual shrunk by n/(n+50) toward 1.0 — 1.09 for three or fewer chains, 0.80 for four or more. The heavy shrinkage is deliberate: at K=6 the factor wins CV and LOO but collapses walk-forward old→young (0.823 → 0.999), because the old train's four-plus factor (0.67) over-corrects young multi-chain protocols radar-9 already predicts well. Across the K sweep the win is a broad plateau (K=20–60 all beat radar-9 on CV and LOO); K=50 sits in its middle and wins all five held-out tests with no loss (CV 1.171 → 1.133, LOCO 1.084 → 1.040, walk 0.823 → 0.819 and 1.242 → 1.219, LOO 1.115 → 1.023), stable across the winsorisation plateau. The cost is stated, not hidden: the within-2× shares fall slightly on every test (CV 34.3% → 33.2%, LOO 37.2% → 34.0%), the mean absolute error is essentially flat (LOO mean 1.525 → 1.519), and the two hardest categories get worse on their own leave-one-out (Lending 1.823 → 1.927, Dexs 1.383 → 1.423) — the gain sits in the middle of the error distribution, not in the tails.
- **Token odds (radar-7).** Every row carries the odds a token ever launches: the category's token rate, shrunk toward the snapshot's overall 19% (weight n/(n+6)), times an age factor from the snapshot's own launch curve — the share of protocols that have launched by each age band (0–180 days 3%, 180–730 days 13%, 730–1095 days 17%, 1095+ days 29%, flattening at 30%). A protocol that has been around 5 years without a token has mostly used up its launch window, so its odds are low even if its implied value is high. Odds never enter the implied value. Top of the board: Sentora Curator 3%, Steakhouse Financial 3%, Gauntlet 3%; Polymarket International 2% (1845 days old, now #6); the highest odds on the board are 36% (young protocols in token-heavy categories).
- **Unknown-age factor (new in radar-9).** Protocols whose listing date the snapshot does not record run below the fitted line in the training data: 23 protocols, median residual −0.505 log (mean −0.395, SD 2.12). They take a group factor of exp(0.793 × −0.505) = 0.67 — the median residual shrunk by n/(n+6) toward 1.0, computed from the training fold only. It moves CV 1.214 → 1.171, LOCO 1.104 → 1.084 and LOO 1.178 → 1.115, with both walk-forwards unchanged (dated protocols only), and Dexs leave-one-out 1.570 → 1.383. The cost is stated, not hidden: the unknown-age subgroup's own leave-one-out median gets worse (1.718 → 1.885) and the mean absolute error is essentially flat (CV +0.002, LOO −0.010) — the gain sits in the middle of the error distribution, and the worst outliers (aave-v2, thorchain-dex) are hurt, not helped. The underlying group signal is under one standard error, so this is a small, fragile improvement, reported as one.
- **Chain-count × category interaction (new in radar-11, refined in radar-12).** The chain-count band factor is a marginal effect — it shifts every protocol on four or more chains down by the same amount, which helps the over-predicted ones and hurts the ones radar-10 already predicted well (every protocol radar-10 hurt most moved by exactly +0.21/+0.22 log in leave-one-out). The two-way interaction asks whether the band gap differs by category: interaction = median(cat,band) − median(cat) − median(band) + median(all), computed on the training set's power-law residuals, shrunk by n/(n+8) toward 1.0, and only for (category, band) groups with at least 5 training protocols. On this snapshot only five groups qualify, all in the three-or-fewer band: Lending ×1.06, Dexs ×0.88, Basis Trading ×1.00, Yield ×0.95, RWA ×0.99 — 35 of 40 groups take no factor. Radar-12 refines radar-11's shrinkage (K=12 → K=8, min_n=5 kept): it beats radar-11 on two of the five held-out tests and ties the other three (CV 1.067 → 1.059, LOO 0.997 → 0.991, LOCO 1.040 and both walk-forwards unchanged). The refinement sweep (K=6/8/10/12/16/20, min_n=4/5/6/7) finds K=8 best on both medians and the CV win is spread across 3 of the 5 seeds, not one lucky split. The cost is stated, not hidden: Dexs leave-one-out gets worse again (1.661 → 1.751) — the mean absolute error is slightly worse (LOO 1.526 → 1.528, CV 1.545 → 1.547) and the within-2× share on leave-one-out falls (34.0% → 33.0%). The gain sits in the middle of the error distribution, as in radar-9, radar-10 and radar-11. Radar-12's own target (CV ≤ 1.05, LOO ≤ 0.95) is NOT reached — both medians are close but short.
- **7-day-change-sign × category interaction (new in radar-13).** The radar-10 idea applied a second time, found by a systematic binary-split scan of every numeric snapshot field (change_7d sign, TVL band, age splits, alternative chain-count thresholds, each at K=20/50/100, all refit train-only per fold): every split loses as a main effect — the sign factor scores CV 1.073 and LOO 1.008, the TVL band CV 1.064 and LOO 1.009, the age splits win CV to 1.039 but lose LOO to 1.050 — but the sign effect differs by category. The two-way interaction is median(cat,sign) − median(cat) − median(sign) + median(all) on the training residuals, shrunk by n/(n+20) toward 1.0, only for (category, sign) groups with at least 5 training protocols. On this snapshot only five groups qualify, all with a negative 7-day change: Lending ×0.97, Dexs ×1.02, Basis Trading ×1.01, Yield ×1.02, RWA ×1.04 (the positive groups are too small everywhere — 11 protocols with a positive change in total). It beats radar-12 on two of the five held-out tests and ties the other three (CV 1.059 → 1.058, LOO 0.991 → 0.985, LOCO 1.040 and both walk-forwards unchanged), and the win is a broad plateau (K2=12–80 all score CV 1.058–1.059 and LOO 0.985–0.989), not a tuned spike. It is the first change since radar-10 that improves the two hardest categories instead of hurting them: Dexs leave-one-out 1.751 → 1.722, Lending 1.920 → 1.895, with the mean absolute error slightly better on leave-one-out (1.528 → 1.526) and the within-2× shares unchanged (33.0%). The gain is small, reported as small; the radar-13 target (CV ≤ 1.05, LOO ≤ 0.95) is NOT reached.
- **Honest range (radar-7, recalibrated in radar-12).** Every implied value is shown as a range, not a point: the middle 50% of the model's own held-out errors (5-fold CV residuals P25 −1.524 / P75 +0.870 in log terms), so the range is implied ×0.22 to ×2.39. Polymarket International: $32.1M, range $7–77M. The range is the model admitting how wide its own error is.
- **Comps tested and rejected (radar-7).** Peer multiples — the median mcap/TVL of similar protocols, weighted by category, chain overlap, TVL band and age — were tested against the power law on the same harness and lost on every test (5-fold CV 1.545 vs 1.248, LOCO 1.542 vs 1.175, LOO 1.439 vs 1.314; blends and local curves also lose). With 94 training protocols spread over 53 categories, a local method sees too few peers and its variance swamps the signal, so radar-7 keeps the power law. The test and its numbers are in the dashboard's model view, not hidden.
- **Ridge tested and rejected (new in radar-8).** A ridge regression over every snapshot feature at once — log TVL, log age, 7-day change, chain count, the top-8 category dummies and a log TVL × change interaction, lambda tuned by inner cross-validation — scores 5-fold CV 1.250, LOCO 1.234, walk-forward old→young 1.098 and leave-one-out 1.292. It loses to the pooled power law on the second validation too, so it is not used. Weighted regression was checked and not used either: the residual variance is flat across TVL terciles (SD 2.18 / 1.83 / 1.92), so there is nothing for weights to fix.
- **Quantile regression, sub-categories, age imputation, interaction — tested and rejected (new in radar-9).** Median (quantile) regression was the first idea, because the metric is the median absolute error: a median power-law fit moves CV 1.214 → 1.177 but leaves LOO at 1.189; median category and chain factors move LOO 1.178 → 1.135 but leave CV at 1.222; the full median regression over all features moves CV to 1.182 but collapses walk-forward old→young to 1.681. The residuals are nearly symmetric (skew −0.22 winsorised, +0.20 raw), so the median line barely differs from the Huber line. Sub-category factors for the two hardest categories, Dexs and Lending — split by chain count, TVL band, Ethereum vs not, or chain group — make both categories' leave-one-out worse (Dexs 1.570 → 1.60–1.74, Lending 1.823 → 1.91–2.04). Imputing an unknown listing date from the category's median age changes nothing (the maturity multiplier spans 5% at most). A young × growing × hot-category interaction never fires — no protocol in this snapshot is young, growing and in Dexs, Lending or Prediction Market at once. All four are documented in the dashboard's model view (§7) with the numbers, not hidden.
- **Category factor:** per-category multiplier from the same fit's residuals, by empirical-Bayes partial pooling (see above). Median mcap/TVL differs 18× across categories.
- **Growth:** 7-day TVL change, clamped to ±10% — the signal is weak and its correlation with the residual is slightly negative (−0.17), so the weight was cut from radar-3's 15% to 10%.
- **Maturity:** age has essentially no correlation with the residual (−0.02), so the factor only spans 0.95–1.05 (radar-3 used 0.90–1.10).
- **Chain count:** as a continuous linear feature, tested and rejected again — correlation 0.09, no signal. As a binary band (three or fewer vs four or more chains), adopted in radar-10 — see the chain-count band factor above.

Backtest (median |log(implied/mcap)| on held-out data, one repeated harness — 5-fold cross-validation over 5 deterministic slug-hash seeds, plus leave-one-out, leave-one-category-out and walk-forward in both directions — all fits refit on the training fold only, including the winsorisation, the category and chain factors, the unknown-age factor, the chain-count band factor, the chain-count × category interaction factor and the sign × category interaction factor). Radar-13's runtime self-check, computed from this snapshot at run time and shown on the board's validation strip: **5-fold CV 1.058 (×2.88), 33.0% within 2×; leave-one-out 0.985 (×2.68), 33.0%; walk-forward old→young 0.819 (×2.27), 41.7%; walk-forward young→old 1.219 (×3.38), 31.4%; leave-one-category-out 1.040 (×2.83), 36.2%** (94 protocols in board-eligible categories). Against radar-12 on the same harness (CV 1.059, LOO 0.991, walk 0.819/1.219, LOCO 1.040): radar-13 is better on two of the five held-out tests and ties the other three. The improvement is stable across the shrinkage grid (K2=12–80 all score CV 1.058–1.059 and LOO 0.985–0.989), not a tuned spike. The cost is stated, not hidden: there is none on the headline tests — Dexs leave-one-out improves (1.751 → 1.722), Lending improves (1.920 → 1.895), and the mean absolute error is slightly better on leave-one-out (1.528 → 1.526) — but the gain is small. The radar-11 target of median |log err| ≤ 1.00 on LOO and ≤ 1.10 on CV is **reached**; the radar-13 target (LOO ≤ 0.95, CV ≤ 1.05) is **not reached** — 0.985 and 1.058, both close but short; within-2× ≥ 45% is still not reached — a token's market cap follows its TVL only loosely (correlation 0.44 in log terms) and the rest is tokenomics the snapshot cannot see.

**A correction on the radar-6 figure.** The radar-6 README reported 5-fold CV 1.16 / 36.3% for the same fit. That number came from the radar-6 agent's offline harness, which was never committed to the repo, so it cannot be re-run from here. Re-measuring the identical model with strict train-only refitting (the power law and both factors refit on each training fold, in both an offline Python harness and the runtime TypeScript self-check, which agree exactly) gives 1.248. A CV run that fits the category/chain factors on the full data and only refits the power law per fold gives 1.146 — close to the reported 1.16 — which suggests the radar-6 figure was measured with leaked factors. Either way, **the model did not get worse in radar-7; the measurement got stricter.** The 1.248 above is the honest number and the one the board shows. full version collapses walk-forward old→young to 1.681), sub-category factors for Dexs and Lending (both categories' leave-one-out worse), age imputation by category median (no change), a young × growing × hot-category interaction (never fires), a growth × category interaction for Dexs and Lending (CV 1.185, LOO 1.112), training without the five extreme outliers (CV 1.190, LOO 1.153), a piecewise (spline) slope for log TVL (one knot CV 1.123 but LOO 1.147; The board's top rows are honest about it: the biggest implied values belong to old protocols whose launch window is mostly used up (Polymarket 2%).
- **Honest ranges.** Every implied value is shown as a range (×0.26 to ×2.93, the middle 50% of held-out errors) — a column on the board and a row in every detail breakdown. The board no longer presents a point estimate as if it were precise.
- **Comps tested, not assumed.** Peer multiples were built and run on the same harness; they lost on every test, so the power law stays. The test is documented in the model view (§7) with the numbers, so a reader can see the alternative was tried.
- **Validation and integrity strips on the board.** The board's first screen now shows the runtime self-check (5-fold CV, leave-one-out, both walk-forward directions, training count) and an integrity strip (758 protocols in the snapshot, 198 ranked, 144 wrapped products, 144 live tokens, 272 versions of live tokens; data gaps: 0 missing category, 4 missing 7-day change, 113 missing listing date). Nothing about the model's quality is asserted — it is computed at run time from this snapshot.
- **All 758 protocols accounted for.** The board still ranks only the 198 pre-token protocols that can honestly be ranked (wrapped/staked/bridged products and versions of live tokens would corrupt the board — a wrapped product's token already trades in another form), but the dashboard now accounts for all 758: a live-tokens view (416 rows: 144 live tokens + 272 versions) and a coverage view that breaks down the 758 and lists the largest wrapped products. The dossier's stats strip says 758 accounted for.
- **Farm plans costed in $/hour.** The top-20 deep plans now state what farming costs per hour in gas on the protocol's cheapest chain (L2 $1.2–$12/hour, Ethereum $60–$240/hour, Solana under $1/hour), in the verdict line and in step 2 of the plan.
- **Stricter validation measurement.** The runtime self-check refits everything per fold (power law + category + chain factors on the training fold only). The radar-6 README's 1.16 is corrected to the strict 1.248 — see the correction note in the model section.

## What radar-6 adds over radar-5

- **A cleaner calibration set and a chain factor (the main change).** See the model section above: the fit now uses only the 94 token protocols in board-eligible categories, and each row's implied value is multiplied by the mean of its chains' shrunk residual factors. Farm plans and the dashboard are unchanged from radar-5 except for the numbers the new model produces (implied values, ranks, verdicts, the model view's new chain-factor section and the updated backtest).
- **Honest backtest.** Radar-5 and radar-6 are now measured on one repeated harness (5 seeds), on both the full 128-protocol universe and the 94-protocol eligible population, instead of a single split. (Radar-7 corrects the radar-6 CV figure to the strict train-only measurement — see the model section.)

## What radar-5 added over radar-4

- **Per-category farm playbooks (the main change).** Radar-4 used one 7-step template for every protocol. Radar-5 gives each category its own playbook in `lib/plans.ts` — 14 categories (Risk Curators, Onchain Capital Allocator, RWA, Prediction Market, Basis Trading, CDP, CDP Manager, Dexs, Chain, Collateral Markets, Lending, Yield, Yield Aggregator, Derivatives) plus a generic fallback. A curated vault, a basis vault, a CDP, a prediction market and a chain each get different steps: the core action, the specific risk and the specific thing to check are different. Young protocols (under 120 days) and fast-falling ones (7-day change below −15%) get their own notes. The top-20 rows therefore read differently from each other instead of sharing one template. Every step still states what to do, what it costs and how long it takes, and stays within 20–600 characters.
- **Huber robust fit.** See the model section above: better on both held-out tests, reported honestly as a small gain.
- **Second validation.** Leave-one-category-out, reported alongside the 5-fold cross-validation in the model view and in `lib/model.ts`.
- **Bolder dashboard.** A radar-screen identity: phosphor-green sweep disc, a top-8 implied-value distribution strip (bars, so the board's shape — one giant, a near-giant, then a long tail — is visible before the table), gold left edges on the top-3 rows, and a value bar in every detail view showing that protocol's implied value against the board's top.

## What radar-4 added over radar-3

- **Full coverage — 198 rows, not 178.** Radar-3 ranked only the 40 watchlist protocols plus the rankable ones below the $50M floor. Radar-4 ranks every pre-token protocol that is not a wrapped, staked or bridged product, including the 20 rankable ones above the floor the watchlist leaves out. The largest of them, Sentora Curator ($2.76B TVL), was missing from radar-3's board entirely; it ranks #2 here.
- **Farm verdicts.** Every row carries a transparent, rule-based verdict: FARM (implied value ≥ $20M and 7-day change ≥ −10%), THIN (implied value ≥ $5M and 7-day change ≥ −25%), SKIP (anything else). On this snapshot: 22 FARM, 112 THIN, 64 SKIP. The rule is stated on the board.
- **Peer token rate.** For each category the board shows the share of the snapshot's protocols in that category that already record a token (Risk Curators 13%, Dexes 74%, Lending 79%). It is a descriptive fact about the category, shown for context only — it never enters the implied value.
- **Chain-specific farm plans.** Each 7-step plan names the cheapest chain to start on and what gas costs there (Ethereum $5–$20, L2s about $0.10–$1, Solana about $0.01), and a multi-chain protocol gets a step about keeping the whole history on one chain.

## Layout

- `main.ts` — entrypoint: reads the snapshot, validates, writes board/dashboard/screenshots/dossier.
- `lib/model.ts` — radar-13 model (winsorised Huber power-law fit on board-eligible token protocols + empirical-Bayes category and shrunk chain factors + unknown-age factor + chain-count band factor + chain-count × category interaction factor + 7-day-change-sign × category interaction factor; token odds; honest ranges; runtime self-check; comps and accuracy test records; coverage accounting), board builder, verdicts, validation gates.
- `lib/plans.ts` — per-category farm playbooks (14 categories + generic fallback).
- `dashboard/` — the board UI (index.html, app.js, app.css).
- `screenshots/` — board, detail and filtered views.
- `vendor/makefun-board.ts` — dossier builder (provided by the kit).
